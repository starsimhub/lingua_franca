# odin age-structured SEIR with a delay, in Starsim

The translation of [examples/odin/seir_array](../../../examples/odin/seir_array), odin's wave 3 example. The model is `model.py`; `compare.py` checks it against the original's reference results.

## Comparison

| Measure | Original | Starsim | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines of model code | 53 | 17 | No line is over 100 characters |
| One run | 1.16 s as a command, of which the run is 0.002 s | 2.2 s, of which the run is 1.9 s | The original's first run is mostly compiling the C code. Its run of the delay and Starsim's conveyor are both quick |
| Results, in total | | | **Agree.** `S`, `E`, `I`, and `R` within 0.23%, 0.36%, 0.25%, and 0.15% of their largest values on every day (a quarter of that with a step of half a day), and `I` peaks at 6.19 million on day 125, as in the original. It needs the workaround of finding 1: without it the epidemic peaks 15 days late. `compare.py` allows 0.5% |
| Results, by age | | | **Agree, except in the youngest group,** where `E`, `I`, and `R` differ by up to 15%, 5%, and 8%. The original's delay does not let the latent age (finding 2); Starsim's does. `compare.py` allows 1% in the other groups |

Measured on 14 October 2026 with Starsim 4.0.0 (the `v4` branch) on a 20-core x86-64 Linux machine.

## What was declared and what was not

Everything is declared: seven transitions and one stratification, whose aging between groups is generated, and the delay is a fixed dwell time. The stable age distribution is worked out by hand in two lines of NumPy, as the original works it out in two lines of odin (`den[1]` and `den[2:N_age]`). One transition, `history`, is a workaround (finding 1).

| Original | Starsim |
|---|---|
| `dim(S) <- N_age` and so on, for 9 arrays | `seir.stratify(age=[0, 1, 5, 15, 30], split=split)` |
| `age_width`, `age_rate`, and the aging terms in all eight `deriv()` equations | Generated by the age stratification, at one over each width |
| `new_inf[] <- beta * S[i] * I_tot / N` with `I_tot <- sum(I)` | `S >> E @ ss.perday(1) * I` |
| `lag_inf[] <- delay(new_inf[i] * surv, lat_hum)` | `E >> I @ ss.constant(ss.days(14))`, with deaths from `E` doing the work of `surv` |
| `Births <- b * N` into `S[1]`, and `- b * X[i]` in every equation | `ss.birth(S, ...)` and `ss.death(...)` |
| `den[]` | `split`, worked out the same way |
| `delay()`'s value before day 14 | `history`, an inflow to `I` for 14 days (finding 1) |

## Findings

1. **The history of a delay (blocks agreement; worked around).** For the first 14 days, odin's `delay(new_inf * surv, 14)` looks back to before day 0 and takes the value at day 0, so about one person a day reaches `I` as if infections had been going on before the start. A model whose latent state starts empty cannot do this, and with a reproduction number near 30, the 14 extra cases bring the epidemic forward by 15 days (without them, `I` differs by up to 66% of its peak). The workaround is a transition that adds them: `history = ss.null >> I @ ss.timeseries({0: ss.perday(surv), 14: 0})`. It adds 14 people that the original takes from `E`, a difference of one part in a million. Suggested change: let `init_n` fill a fixed delay as if people had been arriving at a constant rate (for example `init_n=dict(E=ss.arrived(rate=1))`), since the conveyor below has a natural place for them.

2. **The original's latent do not age (disagreement by age, not a Starsim issue).** In the original, `E` is aged like every state, but `lag_inf[i]` takes from `E[i]` everyone infected in group `i` 14 days earlier, including the 1 in 26 of the youngest who have aged out since. So `E[1]` falls short and `I[1]` gets more than it should; summed over ages nothing is lost. Starsim's conveyor ages with everyone else. This was checked with a separate Python version of the original's equations: as written, it matches the reference to within 0.2% in every group; with the latent aging, it differs from the reference in the youngest group by about what Starsim does. Both are reasonable, and the difference comes from how the original is written.

3. **Fixed: a fixed delay was many stages, which were slow and still approximate.** The delay used to be 200 Erlang stages, which took 1.6 s a run and was 1.6% off in `I`. A fixed dwell time, `ss.constant(ss.days(14))`, is now a conveyor of whole steps under every solver: people who enter in a step wait in one slot for each step of the delay, thinned by deaths and moved along by aging. It is exact at the step, is not stiff, and takes milliseconds. The largest differences fall by about four times when the step is halved.

4. **Fixed: the first age group was labeled `0-0`.** An age group one year wide is now labeled by its single year, so the groups are `0, 1-4, 5-14, 15-29, 30+`, and `compare.py` selects by label.

5. **`describe()` leaves out aging (half fixed).** Inflows are now printed as inflows, with their rate. Still open: the aging transfers generated by the age stratification are not listed, though spec 05 says `describe()` lists them, so nothing in the output says that people age, or at what rates.

6. **Fixed: assigning a distribution to a transition after it was made broke the build.** Assigning `dur` now sets the value of the transition's parameter. One thing to know: `ss.Sim(seir)` keeps its own copy of the disease, so assigning `seir.onset.dur` after the sim is made changes nothing in that sim, silently. It would be clearer if the sim either used the disease it was given or said that it had copied it.
